Fe XIII Stokes Explorer

Place a parcel of coronal plasma above the Sun, orient its magnetic field, and watch the polarized emission of Fe XIII 1074.7 nm and 1079.8 nm respond. Atomic level populations and alignments come from a pre-computed pyCELP table; the viewing geometry and the spectra are evaluated live in your browser.

One point, not a line of sightwhy real observations differ

Like single-point inversions such as CLEDB, the page treats all the light as coming from one point. A real coronal line of sight crosses plasma with different densities, temperatures and field directions, and the observed profiles are emissivity-weighted sums along it. Linear polarization from differently oriented fields partly cancels, V averages BLOS, the 1079/1074 ratio leans toward the densest plasma, and the K corona collects every electron. These effects are often not negligible.

How the page computes the profiles

What is pre-computed

For each line, the polarized emission needs two numbers from the statistical equilibrium: the population term C = (hν/4π) A Nu and the upper-level alignment σ20 = ρ20/ρ00. pyCELP's Ion.calc_rho_sym gives both as functions of ne, Te, height and θB.

Degeneracies that keep the table small

  • The radiation-field anisotropy enters as P2(cos θB), so only cos²θB matters: θB and 180° − θB are identical, and 11 nodes in cos²θB interpolate almost exactly.
  • |B|, the line of sight and the Q/U reference never enter the statistical equilibrium (no atomic coherences, saturated Hanle regime). Moving the voxel around the Sun at fixed height and inclination reuses the same entry.
  • Ambiguous twin is the irreducible two-line degeneracy found by Paraschiv & Judge (2022) with CLEDB: mirroring the voxel across the plane of the sky and turning B⊥ by 180°, keeping BLOS, leaves the height, cos²θB, ΘB and ψB mod 180° unchanged. No combination of the two lines' I, Q, U, V can tell the pair apart. In the plane of the sky it is the 180° azimuth ambiguity.
  • Reverse B flips only Stokes V. Mirror keeps θB and ΘB and flips ψB, which flips U in the radial frame.

Emission coefficients (Schad & Dima 2020, Eqs. 3, 6–8)

εI = C [1 + 3⁄2√2 D σ20 (cos²ΘB − ⅓)] φ(λ)
εQ′ = C 3⁄2√2 D σ20 sin²ΘB φ(λ)
εQ = −εQ′ cos 2ψB,  εU = −εQ′ sin 2ψB
εV = −(λ²/c) C cos ΘB νL (ḡ + E σ20) φ′(λ)

εQ′ = I(⊥B⊥) − I(∥B⊥): with σ20 > 0 the angular pattern of εI is that of the σ components, which emit polarization perpendicular to the field. pyCELP writes εQ = εQ′ cos 2γB, εU = −εQ′ sin 2γB, so the page passes azimuthBLOSdeg = γB = 90° − ψB.

The profile φ is a Gaussian with thermal width at Te plus the non-thermal velocity, as in emissionLine.calc_stokesSpec. The browser code is a line-by-line port of pyCELP and matches it to 10−6.

Conventions and limits

  • +x points to the observer, +z is solar north, +y is solar west. The line of sight is taken parallel to +x.
  • ψB is the counter-clockwise rotation, as seen by the observer, from the +Q reference to the plane-of-sky field. The polarization angle χ = ½ atan2(U, Q) is measured the same way (IAU sense).
  • Radial reference: Q < 0 means polarization tangential to the limb. Solar north: a fixed frame for all positions, angles measured north through east.
  • pyCELP assumes the saturated Hanle regime, valid for these lines above ~10−5 G: the alignment is quantized along B, so Q and U follow the field direction at any strength and |B| enters only V. At |B| = 0 there is no field direction. The page then aligns the atoms along the local vertical, the symmetry axis of the photospheric radiation, which gives the radial-field result with V = 0. The unsaturated Hanle regime between 0 and ~10−5 G is not modelled.
  • The profiles are emission coefficients of one voxel (a single emitting volume element), not line-of-sight integrals, which is the single-point picture behind inversions such as CLEDB. Observed profiles sum the emission of every voxel along the line of sight, weighted by its emissivity. That mixes field directions, which reduces and rotates the net linear polarization, most strongly where some voxels lie beyond the Van Vleck angle and others do not. It averages BLOS in V, and weights the density from the 1079/1074 ratio toward the brightest plasma. Occultation and the disk background are not applied to the spectra.

Observation effects (optional)

The Observation effects card turns the coronal profiles into a schematic observed spectrum, following the DKIST/Cryo-NIRSP spectral model of T. Schad. Effects apply in the order light meets them:

  • K corona: this voxel's Thomson scattering, as in pyCELP's PSI synthesis example. With σT = 0.665 × 10−24 cm², and χ the angle between the local vertical and the line of sight:
    • εI = σTneJ00(1 + ω/2 − ¾ω sin²χ)
    • εpB = −¾σTneωJ00 sin²χ, which is Q in the radial frame.
    J00 and the anisotropy ω come from pycelp.allenSolRad. The scale slider multiplies both.
  • Scattered light from the sky and the instrument is added next. It uses the disk-integrated solar spectrum S(λ), with its continuum set to a multiple a of the coronal 1074.7 nm I peak, the same for both lines. Its V and Q/U are weak-field Zeeman signals, with C = 4.6686 × 10−13 Å−1 G−1 and one effective field for all photospheric lines:
    • V = −Cλ²ḡBLOS a dS/dλ
    • Q + iU = −¼(Cλ²B⊥)² e2iφ a d²S/dλ²
  • Telluric absorption then multiplies all four Stokes parameters by T(λ)scale.
  • Crosstalk mixes the totals: Q′ = Q + xIQ I + xVQ V, U′ = U + xIU I + xVU V, V′ = V + xIV I + xQV Q + xUV U.
  • Spectrograph resolution applies last, as a Gaussian of FWHM λ/R.

Scattered-light polarization and crosstalk are fixed on the sky, in the solar-north frame, and are rotated into the radial frame when that reference is selected. The atlases are placed relative to the rest air wavelengths used for Cryo-NIRSP data, 1074.65 and 1079.80 nm. The coronal lines sit there plus their Doppler shift. Not modelled:

  • noise;
  • the F corona, which near the Sun behaves like more scattered light;
  • velocity shifts of the atlases;
  • per-line amplitudes of the scattered-light Zeeman signals.

The starting values are rounded from a fit to Cryo-NIRSP 1074.7 nm data.
